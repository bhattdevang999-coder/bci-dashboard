# NOVELLE — Brand Brief

**Purpose:** Source of truth for the Novelle brand identity. Read this before generating any Novelle-facing content (listings, ads, A+ Content, Brand Story panels, social, video scripts, packaging copy). Every output should be defensible against this document.

**Status:** Working brief. Authored May 27, 2026. Stress-tested before commit, but not final — version history at bottom.

---

## Positioning (locked, May 26-27 2026)

Novelle is the **honest premium** position in the activewear category. Same factory, same materials, same craftsmanship as brands you trust — without the brand tax.

**One-line positioning:** *"Novelle launched in late 2025 with one goal: same quality, zero brand tax. Try us once. See what we mean."*

**Strategic frame (Italic/Quince/Everlane playbook adapted):**
- We sell premium-spec product at honest pricing
- We don't pretend to be luxury — we cut the luxury markup
- Trust transfer comes from: 30-year NY-founded brand + factory that supplies major global fashion retailers + (pending) OEKO-TEX certification
- The price isn't a discount — it's the absence of brand tax

**What we are NOT:**
- Not luxury (don't claim premium-tier brand status — claim premium-tier specs at fair price)
- Not Lululemon (too aspirational, too logo-forward)
- Not Alo (too LA, too celebrity-adjacent)
- Not Aerie (too young, too pastel)
- Not Gymshark (too fitness-bro, too transactional)
- Not Patagonia (wrong category emotionally)
- Not Beyond Yoga (too earnest, no edge)
- Not Athleta (too suburban-mom, too department-store)

## Brand archetype

**Primary: The Sage**

Quiet authority. Knows what she wants. Doesn't sell you on becoming someone — offers something true and lets you find your meaning in it. *"She doesn't explain herself"* is pure Sage voice. *"Come to me"* is the Sage's invitation: confident, low-pressure, knowing.

**Secondary: The Rebel (undertones)**

The "no brand tax" position is quietly anti-establishment. Not screaming about it. Just refusing to play the markup game.

**Design implications of this archetype combination:**
- Minimalist composition
- Restraint with color
- Editorial photography (NOT commercial)
- Confident typography
- Generous whitespace
- No theatrics, no exclamation points, no "transformation" language

**Reference brands with this archetype:** Toteme, The Row, Aesop, Le Labo, Lemaire, early Outdoor Voices (2018-2020)

## The Novelle woman

She's 28-42. She lives in New York or thinks like she does — Brooklyn, Manhattan, women who pass through SoHo on Saturday morning without making a scene about it.

She works in something demanding (creative, finance, product, founder, lawyer). She has a yoga practice or a Pilates routine or runs the Hudson — usually quietly, not for Instagram. She shows up exactly on time, knows the wine, doesn't need to be the loudest in the room.

She wears Aritzia, Toteme, The Row, vintage Levi's, and *some* Lululemon — but Lulu's logo bothers her slightly. She has read enough about Bangladesh garment workers to want to know where her clothes are made. She's not interested in performance theater (squat selfies, "wellness journey" content). She's interested in *the actual product*.

She's tired of paying $128 for leggings because of the logo. She's also tired of $24 leggings that wear out in three months. She's been waiting for someone to do the in-between honestly and not insult her intelligence about it.

When she finds Novelle, she doesn't tell anyone. She just wears them. If a friend asks, she says "they're good — and they're $27." She likes that nobody else knows about them yet. She'd hate if Novelle became too popular too fast.

**Designing for her:** restraint over flash, specificity over hype, quiet over loud.

## Brand voice — locked anchors

**Hero line:** *She doesn't explain herself.*

**Closer:** *Come to me.*

**Mission closer (new, locked May 27 2026):** *Novelle launched in late 2025 with one goal: same quality, zero brand tax. Try us once. See what we mean.*

**Trust transfer line:** *Same factory. Same materials. Same craftsmanship. Without the brand tax.*

**NY anchoring chapters:**
- Brooklyn yoga studio (morning)
- SoHo cobblestones (midday)
- West Village window seat (evening)

**Voice principles:**
- Direct, never overstated
- Specific, never generic
- Confident, never aggressive
- Knowing, never preachy
- Quiet, never theatrical
- Allows the reader to draw her own conclusion

**Tonal no-fly zones:**
- No exclamation points
- No "girlboss" tone
- No "transformation" framing
- No "before and after"
- No emoji except in specific UI cues (e.g., "❤ Add to wishlist")
- No fitness-jargon ("crushed it," "beast mode," "gains")
- No wellness-speak ("manifest," "intention," "journey")

## Visual references — the moodboard

### Brands in our DNA (ranked by relevance)

1. **Aritzia** — primary reference. NY-editorial sensibility, restraint, premium-but-not-luxury, women's confidence.
2. **Toteme** — Scandi-minimalist, monochromatic palettes, photography that lets the product breathe.
3. **Outdoor Voices (2018-2020 era)** — warmth and friendliness. *Avoid current OV.*
4. **Vuori (Amazon listings specifically)** — for cinematic lifestyle treatment.
5. **Le Labo / Aesop (brand surfaces, not products)** — for typographic restraint and confidence in negative space.

### Photographers whose visual language should inform direction

- **Petra Collins** — natural light, intimate, female gaze, slightly nostalgic.
- **Brigitte Lacombe** — editorial portraiture, NY sensibility, women shot with confidence not glamour.
- **Ryan McGinley** — natural light, movement, youth without being childish.

### Films that capture the brand mood

- **Frances Ha** (Noah Baumbach) — NY at the right scale, women in motion, natural light, restraint.
- **Lost in Translation** (Sofia Coppola) — dusk light, quiet luxury, unspoken-emotion-as-aesthetic.
- **Past Lives** (Celine Song) — NY at dusk, women carrying weight quietly, gold-hour palette.

## Brand color system

Beyond the 4 product colorways:

| Role | Color name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary ink | Deep Ink Black | `#1A1A1A` | Headlines, primary type, logo on light bg |
| Background cream | Warm Cream | `#F5F1E9` | Background fields, negative space (NOT pure white) |
| Mid-neutral | Warm Stone | `#A89E91` | Secondary type, dividers, supporting copy |
| Primary accent | Burnt Sienna | `#A05A2C` | Accent moments, hover states, brand banner moments |
| Quiet accent | Dusk Lavender | `#8A7E94` | Secondary accent, occasional brand-mood color |

**Product colorways (for SKU-specific marketing):**

| Color | Hex | Brand chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Black | `#0F0F10` | The foundation |
| Charcoal | `#3A3D40` | The understated one |
| Dark Navy | `#1F2B43` | Quiet confidence |
| Deep Olive | `#3F4A2E` | The one that surprises you |

**Palette rules:**
- The brand's dominant marketing palette is **cream + ink + warm stone**, with the colorways shown as product-specific within that frame
- Pure white (`#FFFFFF`) is for Amazon main-image compliance only — never as a brand surface
- No bright primary colors (red, royal blue, yellow are off-brand)
- Burnt Sienna is the only "warm" accent — use sparingly, single-element-per-surface

## Typography

**Primary system (for digital, image overlays, and design assets):**

- **Headlines / display:** GT America (Bold/Medium) — modern NY-editorial sans
- **Body:** Inter (Regular/Medium) — clean reliable workhorse

**Alternative for editorial moments (Storefront, future DTC site):**

- **Display:** Tiempos Headline — for editorial premium moments
- **Body:** Söhne — for editorial pairing

**Avoid:**
- Trendy display fonts (Brandon Grotesque heavy, Gotham Black, Comfortaa)
- Serif body text on digital
- Mixed-weight scripts
- Anything that signals "marketing department"

**Type hierarchy:**

| Role | Font | Size range |
|---|---|---|
| Display (hero) | GT America Bold | 48-72pt |
| Headline | GT America Medium | 28-36pt |
| Subhead | GT America Regular | 20-24pt |
| Body | Inter Regular | 14-16pt |
| Caption | Inter Regular | 11-13pt |

Generous letter-spacing on display (creates intentional, confident wordmark feel).

## Founders' emotional why

Working draft of the founder voice (to be refined by Devang):

> *We've been making clothes for 30 years. We've watched brands charge $128 for what costs $14 to make. We've watched customers pay it because they didn't know. We launched Novelle because we wanted to do one thing: deliver the product, not the markup. Try us once. You'll see what we mean.*

**Voice principles:** Industry-veteran honesty. Quiet conviction. Not corporate, not apologetic, not flashy. People-who-know-the-game telling you they're playing it differently.

**Note:** No founder face/photo on any brand surface. The brand is the brand — humans stay behind it.

## Compliance and claim discipline

**Claims we can make:**
- "Made by a 30-year manufacturer that produces for major global fashion retailers"
- "Designed in New York"
- "OEKO-TEX certified" *(only after factory certificate confirmed and on file)*
- Specific construction claims with measurements (5″ waistband, 79/21 nylon-spandex, etc.)

**Claims we cannot make:**
- "Same as Lululemon/Vuori/Alo" (names a competitor — Amazon policy and contractual issue)
- "Made by the factory of luxury brands" (factory makes for H&M, Cato, CRZ — not luxury)
- "Premium luxury quality" (premium feel ≠ luxury — overreach)
- Vague quality claims without backing ("impeccable," "best-in-class," "A1")
- Any "guaranteed" or "100%" language without proof
